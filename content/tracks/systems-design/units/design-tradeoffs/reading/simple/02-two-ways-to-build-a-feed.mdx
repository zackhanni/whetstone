title: Two ways to build a feed — do the work on write or on read
---

A home feed shows recent posts from the accounts you follow. There are two places to do the
merging:

**Fan-out on write (push).** When someone posts, copy a reference to the post into the stored
timeline of *every follower*. Reading your feed is then one cheap lookup of a list that's already
built.

```
post by @ana (300 followers)  →  300 inserts, one into each follower's timeline
open my feed                   →  read my timeline: one query, already sorted
```

**Fan-out on read (pull).** Store each post once. When you open your feed, fetch recent posts from
every account you follow and merge them on the spot.

```
post by @ana   →  1 insert
open my feed   →  query recent posts of the 200 accounts I follow, merge, sort, cut to 50
```

Each one puts the cost where the other saves it:

| | Fan-out on write | Fan-out on read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost of a post | one write per follower | one write |
| Cost of opening the feed | one read | one read per followed account, plus a merge |
| A post appears in feeds | after the fan-out finishes | immediately |
| Worst case | an account with millions of followers | a user who follows thousands of accounts |

The precomputed timeline is a **materialized view**: Azure's pattern describes exactly this — a
prepopulated, read-only view built for one query, maintained when the source changes, and
"completely disposable", because it can be rebuilt from the posts and the follow graph. Thinking
of it that way answers some questions for free: a lost timeline is rebuilt, not restored; and a
timeline is a *cache* of the feed query, with all of a cache's staleness.

Which one wins is decided by numbers — how many reads per write, and how lopsided the follower
counts are. The medium tier does that arithmetic.
