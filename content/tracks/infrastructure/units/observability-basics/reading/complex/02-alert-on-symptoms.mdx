id: alert-on-symptoms
title: Alert on what users feel, not on what might cause it
---

Google's SRE book frames monitoring as two questions: *what's broken* (the **symptom**) and
*why* (the **cause**). Pages should fire on symptoms. "Checkout error rate is 4%" is a symptom:
users are hurting right now, whatever the reason. "CPU is at 90%" is a possible cause: maybe
it hurts users, maybe it's a batch job doing exactly what it should at 3 a.m.

### Which symptoms

The SRE book's **four golden signals** for a user-facing system:

- **Latency** — and track failed requests' latency separately: a fast error flatters the
  average, and a slow error is worse than a fast one.
- **Traffic** — demand, e.g. requests per second.
- **Errors** — the rate of failed requests: explicit ones (HTTP 500s), implicit ones (a 200
  with the wrong content) and ones you define by policy ("slower than 1 s counts as an error").
- **Saturation** — how full the most constrained resource is; many systems degrade before
  100%.

Grafana's dashboard guidance names two narrower checklists: **RED** (Rate, Errors, Duration)
for each *service*, and **USE** (Utilization, Saturation, Errors) for each *resource* — CPU,
memory, disks, network. In Grafana's words, RED reports symptoms and USE reports causes, so
alerting belongs on RED; USE is where you look next, to explain why.

### When to page: the error budget

An SLO turns "errors are bad" into a number: *99.9% of checkout requests succeed over 30 days*.
The remaining 0.1% is the **error budget**. The naive alert — "error rate above 0.1% for 10
minutes" — is noisy: the SRE workbook points out that such a 10-minute blip consumes only
0.02% of the month's budget, yet it pages someone.

The workbook alerts on **burn rate** instead — how many times faster than "exactly on budget"
you're spending it — over two windows at once, and its suggested starting points are:

| Burn rate | Long window | Short window | Budget spent | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14.4 | 1 hour | 5 minutes | 2% | page |
| 6 | 6 hours | 30 minutes | 5% | page |
| 1 | 3 days | 6 hours | 10% | ticket |

The long window makes an alert significant; the short window (about 1/12 of the long one) makes
it stop soon after the problem does. A fast, severe outage pages within minutes; a slow leak
becomes a ticket instead of a 3 a.m. phone call.

<!--more-->

**Every page should be actionable.** The SRE book's test: if the right response to a page is
robotic, it shouldn't be a page — automate it or delete it. Cause-based alerts often fail that
test: disk at 80% on a volume that grows 1% a month is a ticket, not a wake-up. Keep them as
dashboard panels and investigation aids, and let symptoms decide who gets woken.
