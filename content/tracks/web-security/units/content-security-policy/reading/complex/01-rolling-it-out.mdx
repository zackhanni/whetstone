---
id: rolling-it-out
title: Rolling a policy out without breaking production
---

An enforced policy that's wrong breaks the site: a blocked analytics loader, a payment iframe that
never appears, a chunk that won't load. The way in is to **report first, enforce second**.

**Report-Only.** The same policy sent under a different header name is evaluated but not enforced
— MDN: "The policy is not enforced, but any violations are sent to the reporting endpoint
specified in the policy."

```http
Reporting-Endpoints: csp-endpoint="https://example.com/csp-reports"
Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only: script-src 'nonce-R' 'strict-dynamic'; object-src 'none'; base-uri 'none'; report-to csp-endpoint
```

`Reporting-Endpoints` names an endpoint; `report-to` in the policy points at it. Browsers `POST`
reports as `application/reports+json` with `"type": "csp-violation"`. The older `report-uri`
directive is deprecated, but MDN advises declaring both until `report-to` works everywhere —
browsers that support `report-to` ignore `report-uri`. (MDN's compat data lists `report-to` support
in Firefox only from version 149.)

Both headers can be sent at once, and both are honoured: keep enforcing the policy you trust while
the stricter one only reports. Fix or allow each legitimate violation the reports show; when only
violations you intend to block are left, move the strict policy to the enforcing header.

**More than one policy only ever tightens.** If a response carries two enforced CSP headers, a
resource must pass **both**. MDN: adding policies "can only further restrict the capabilities of
the protected resource". A CDN or platform that adds its own CSP header on top of yours can block
what your policy allows.

**The `<meta>` tag is a partial substitute.** The CSP spec: Report-Only isn't supported in a meta
element, and "Neither are the `report-uri`, `frame-ancestors`, and `sandbox` directives". A
`frame-ancestors 'none'` in a meta tag protects against nothing.

**Test the policy where it actually runs.** The most common way to ship a broken policy is to set it
only in production:

```ts
if (process.env.NODE_ENV === "production") res.setHeader("Content-Security-Policy", csp);
```

Every page then works in development, because no policy exists there; the inline script someone
added last week is only blocked for real users. Frameworks also differ between modes — Next.js's
development server needs `'unsafe-eval'` (React uses `eval` for debugging information) while its
production build doesn't. Run the policy in development too (adding only what dev genuinely
needs), and click through a local production build with the policy on before you ship it.
