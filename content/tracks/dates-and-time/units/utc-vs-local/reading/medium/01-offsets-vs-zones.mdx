---
id: offsets-vs-zones
title: An offset is a snapshot, a time zone is a rule
---

Two different things get called "time zone":

- **An offset** is how far a wall clock is from UTC at one moment: `-05:00`, `+09:00`.
- **A time zone** is a named set of rules for a place, from the IANA database:
  `America/New_York`, `Europe/London`. The rules say which offset applies when, including
  daylight saving time and every historical change.

New York is `-05:00` in January and `-04:00` in July. `getTimezoneOffset()` shows the machine's
offset for the instant you ask about, in minutes, with a sign that surprises everyone:

```js
// machine in America/New_York
new Date(2026, 0, 15).getTimezoneOffset(); // 300: UTC is 300 minutes AHEAD of local
new Date(2026, 6, 15).getTimezoneOffset(); // 240: daylight saving time
// machine in Asia/Tokyo: -540 all year
```

It's "minutes to add to local time to get UTC", so it's positive west of UTC, the opposite of
the `-05:00` you'd write. And it depends on the date, so one call doesn't describe the zone.

## Which one to store

- **A past or exact instant** (order placed, message sent): UTC. The offset at that moment
  doesn't matter once you have the instant.
- **A future or repeating wall-clock time** ("Mondays at 09:00 in New York"): the local time
  plus the zone **name**. An offset is wrong half the year:

```js
// stored as 09:00 with offset -05:00 in January, read back in July:
new Date("2026-07-13T09:00:00-05:00")
  .toLocaleTimeString("en-US", { timeZone: "America/New_York", timeStyle: "short" });
// "10:00 AM": the meeting moved an hour
```

Store `{ weekday: "monday", time: "09:00", timeZone: "America/New_York" }` and compute each
occurrence's instant when you need it. Turning a wall time plus zone into an instant is awkward
with `Date`; that is the job of a zone-aware library (the last unit in this track).

<!--more-->

Abbreviations are not zones either. `EST` is strictly the winter offset, though people write it
in July and mean EDT, and `IST` is used for India, Ireland and Israel. Node documents that it supports zone IDs such as
`America/New_York` in `TZ`, and that other abbreviations or aliases are "strongly discouraged
and not guaranteed".
