title: 301, 302, 307, 308 — permanent or temporary, method kept or not
---

All four send the new URL in `Location`. Two questions pick the code:

| | `POST` may become `GET` | method and body kept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **permanent** (update your links) | `301` | `308` |
| **temporary** (keep using the old URL) | `302` | `307` |

The left column is history: browsers came to re-send a redirected `POST` as a `GET`, and RFC 9110
now allows it. The Fetch standard, which browsers and Node's `fetch` follow, does exactly that —
after a 301 or 302, a `POST` becomes a body-less `GET` (other methods are kept). `307` and `308`
exist to promise the method survives.

```http
HTTP/1.1 308 Permanent Redirect
Location: /v2/uploads
```

For a `GET`, 301 and 308 behave alike, as do 302 and 307. For anything else, choose from the right
column — or `303` when you *want* a `GET` (next card).
